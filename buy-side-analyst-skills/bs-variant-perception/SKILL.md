# Variant perception workflow

## Inputs
- Company/ticker, source materials, user thesis if any, consensus/sell-side notes if supplied, price reaction, model, and desired time horizon.

## Process
1. Identify the likely consensus view from supplied sources. If consensus data is not supplied, state that market belief is inferred and lower confidence.
2. Write the differentiated claim in one sentence: “The market thinks X; evidence suggests Y; the monetizable gap is Z over T timeframe.”
3. Build evidence map: direct evidence, indirect evidence, management statements, customer/supplier checks, financial data, and market signals.
4. Quantify the thesis: revenue/margin/FCF impact, multiple impact, downside impact, and timing.
5. Identify why the market may be wrong: stale heuristics, wrong peer set, transitory noise, misunderstood accounting, hidden optionality, cyclical trough/peak, or information lag.
6. Build disconfirmation plan: what data would prove the thesis wrong, when it arrives, and how to monitor it.
7. End with next diligence steps and highest-value missing evidence.

## Output format
- Consensus view
- Variant view
- Evidence table with confidence rating
- Economic impact
- Why now / timing
- Falsifiers
- Next diligence

Use skeptical language. Avoid overclaiming. Prioritize measurable, testable theses over narrative-only theses.
