# Buy-side initiation research workflow

## Inputs to request or detect
- Company/ticker, geography, share class, reporting currency, fiscal year-end.
- User-supplied filings, transcripts, investor presentations, model files, industry notes, expert-call notes, and price/consensus data.
- Investment mandate: long-only, long/short, event-driven, quality growth, value, credit-aware, or other.
- Time horizon, benchmark, position-size constraints, and required memo format.

## Non-negotiables
- Do not fabricate numbers, citations, quotes, market data, consensus figures, or management guidance.
- Separate facts, assumptions, estimates, and opinions.
- Cite or point to the source for every material factual claim when source material is available.
- Flag missing, stale, low-confidence, or potentially biased data.
- This is research assistance, not personalized investment advice.

## Process
1. Create a source map: filings, transcripts, presentations, model, news, expert calls, datasets.
2. Summarize the business in one paragraph: customers, products, revenue model, geography, value chain role, and economic moat.
3. Build segment-level economics: revenue drivers, margin structure, working capital, capex intensity, unit economics, and cyclicality.
4. Identify what the market likely believes: consensus growth/margin/capital returns, common bull/bear arguments, sell-side framing, and current multiple.
5. Form a differentiated thesis using evidence from sources. Include the mechanism, timing, magnitude, and what would falsify it.
6. Analyze financial quality: revenue recognition, gross margin durability, opex leverage, cash conversion, SBC, one-offs, leverage, dilution, and governance.
7. Perform valuation triangulation: multiples, DCF or FCF yield, sum-of-the-parts if relevant, and scenario table.
8. Map catalysts and monitoring items: earnings dates, product launches, pricing changes, regulation, macro inputs, insider activity, and KPI releases.
9. Produce risks and disconfirming evidence with probability/severity.
10. End with a PM-ready recommendation section only when the user asks for one; otherwise end with next research steps.

## Output format
Use these sections:
- Executive view
- Business overview
- Industry structure and competitive position
- Financial model drivers
- Variant perception
- Valuation and scenarios
- Risks, falsifiers, and red flags
- Catalysts and monitoring plan
- Source gaps and next diligence

Keep the executive view under 250 words. Use tables for valuation, catalysts, risks, and evidence quality.
